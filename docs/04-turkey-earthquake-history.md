## 1. Historical Context: A Land Carved by Seismic Cataclysms

Throughout millennia of recorded civilization, the Anatolian peninsula has borne witness to devastating seismic shocks that flattened ancient empires—from Antioch (Antakya, devastated multiple times in 115 AD and 526 AD with $> 250,000$ dead) to Byzantine Constantinople (557 AD) and Ottoman Istanbul (the 1509 "Small Judgment Day" / *Kıyamet-i Suğra*).

## 3. The 1999 Turning Point: Institutional Modernization

The August 17, 1999 Gölcük earthquake ($M_w 7.6$) devastated the industrial heartland of Turkey along the Gulf of İzmit. Striking at 03:02 AM local time with horizontal surface offsets up to $5.5\text{ meters}$, it collapsed thousands of multi-story reinforced concrete residential structures.

### Institutional Reforms Spurred by 1999:
1. **Creation of AFAD (2009)**: Merged the General Directorate of Disaster Affairs, Civil Defense, and Emergency Management Administration into a unified cabinet-level disaster authority under the Ministry of Interior.
2. **Mandatory Earthquake Insurance (DASK)**: Enacted in 2000 to pool structural reconstruction risk.
3. **Seismic Building Codes (TBDY 2007 & TBDY 2018)**: Shifted engineering regulations to performance-based seismic design, high-ductility confinement detailing, and soil-structure resonance matching.
