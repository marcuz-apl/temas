# 5. The February 6, 2023 Kahramanmaraş Earthquake Doublet

**Section**: Part II — Turkey Earthquake History & Tectonic Setting  
**Target Audience**: Structural Engineers, Seismologists & Technical Responders  
**Status**: Core Reference Manual

## 1. An Unprecedented Continental Doublet

On Monday, February 6, 2023, southeastern Turkey and northern Syria were struck not by a single major earthquake with typical aftershocks, but by an **almost unprecedented continental earthquake doublet**: two distinct mega-ruptures occurring on separate, intersecting fault systems within **nine hours of each other**.

```
  04:17:34 TRT (01:17 UTC)                       13:24:49 TRT (10:24 UTC)
  ★ EVENT 1: Pazarcık (Mw 7.8)                   ★ EVENT 2: Elbistan (Mw 7.5)
  ───────────────────────────────────────        ───────────────────────────────────────
  Fault: East Anatolian Fault Zone (EAF)         Fault: Çardak - Sürgü Fault Zone
  Mechanism: Left-Lateral Strike-Slip            Mechanism: Left-Lateral Strike-Slip
  Rupture Length: ~350 - 400 km                  Rupture Length: ~160 - 180 km
  Peak Surface Slip: > 7.5 meters                Peak Surface Slip: > 8.0 meters
  Focal Depth: 8.6 km (Extremely Shallow)        Focal Depth: 7.0 km (Extremely Shallow)
  Coulomb Stress Trigger: Transferred shear stress northward onto the Çardak fault block!
```

---

## 2. Dynamic Rupture Mechanics: The Multi-Fault Cascade

Modern seismic kinematic inversions and space geodetic radar (InSAR) confirmed that Event 1 was not a simple single-fault slip, but a complex **five-segment cascading failure**:

1. **Initiation (Narlı Fault)**: Nucleated at $8.6\text{ km}$ depth on the splay Narlı fault trending north-northeast.
2. **Branch Inversion & Bi-Directional Rupture**: After $\sim 18\text{ seconds}$, the rupture intersected the main East Anatolian Fault trunk at a junction near Pazarcık and split into two simultaneous rupture fronts propagating in opposite directions:
   - **Northeast Front**: Tore through the Erkenek, Pütürge, and Doğanyol segments toward Malatya.
   - **Southwest Front**: Tore southwest through the Amanos segment, driving directly into Antakya (Hatay) with **supershear velocities** exceeding local shear-wave speeds ($V_r > V_s$).
3. **Extreme Ground Acceleration**: Strong-motion stations recorded Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA) exceeding **$2.0g$** ($> 2,000\text{ cm/s}^2$) and Peak Ground Velocity (PGV) exceeding **$150\text{ cm/s}$**, far exceeding the maximum seismic design spectrum of building codes.

---

## 3. The Second Mega-Shock: Coulomb Stress Transfer

At 13:24 TRT, while search-and-rescue teams and residents were inspecting damaged structures from the morning earthquake, Event 2 detonated:

The first rupture had loaded adjacent crustal blocks with intense positive Coulomb stress. The **Çardak Fault**—an east-west trending conjugate fault that had been quiescent for centuries—fractured violently over a length of $160\text{ km}$. 

Because structures throughout Kahramanmaraş, Malatya, and Adıyaman had already suffered non-linear structural degradation and micro-cracking from Event 1, the sudden violent re-shaking of Event 2 caused catastrophic secondary collapses across the region.

---

## 4. Human and Physical Toll

- **Casualties**: Over **53,500 fatalities** in Turkey and $> 6,000$ in northern Syria, with $> 107,000$ injured.
- **Geographic Scope**: 11 Turkish provinces directly impacted (an area larger than the territory of Austria or Portugal).
- **Physical Displacement**: Railway lines, olive groves, and roads were sheared horizontally by up to **$7.5\text{ to }8.2\text{ meters}$**.
- **Geotechnical Failures**: Severe liquefaction across the alluvial plains of the Asi River (Antakya) and Gölbaşı lake basin; widespread rockfalls and lateral spreading.
